Law search and news sentiment

Two smaller parts of the project. The law search finds the articles of the GDPR and the EU AI Act that match a question. The sentiment check reads financial news sentences and says whether they are good, bad or neutral news for an investor.

Search the GDPR and the EU AI Act

Ask in the words the law uses. The search matches words, not meaning, so "automated decision" finds Article 22 better than "can a computer say no".

How it works. Both laws are split into 1,106 passages and indexed with TF-IDF and FAISS. On 37 questions with known answers, the right article is among the top 4 results 81% of the time (89% when you pick one law). With a language model running locally (Ollama), the same search also writes an answer that cites these passages; the free online demo has no language model, so it shows the passages only. This is a search aid, not legal advice.

Financial news sentiment

One sentence per line, up to 64. English news style works best.

The model. TF-IDF word pairs with logistic regression, trained on 3,448 sentences of the Financial PhraseBank (75% annotator agreement). On the 690 held-out sentences it reaches macro-F1 0.82. The fine-tuned DistilBERT model in the project reaches 0.93, but needs PyTorch and more memory than the free online server has, so it runs on a laptop or Colab only.
